What the emulation gets right

The iO's display is a green-only microdisplay behind a diffractive waveguide, so the panel above is monochrome by necessity, not by styling. Every constraint below was measured on the device, and the demo obeys all of them.

115

Characters, hard

A body of 115 characters renders in full; 116–130 is visibly cut; 131 or more never renders at all — while every layer upstream still reports success.

chars

Not bytes

A 107-character frame weighing 152 bytes rendered whole, so glyphs cost nothing beyond their character count.

BMP

Glyphs, not emoji

Basic-Multilingual-Plane symbols render reliably; emoji outside it do not, and are stripped before sending.

3 lines

App · title · body

The relay forwards only those three fields. Custom icons and notification styles are silently dropped.

Drawing with a green-only display

With no colour and three lines of text, the only way to draw is with characters. These are the glyphs the assistant uses, and the level bar it builds from block characters for timers.

How a question becomes a card

The glasses have no camera and no direct link to a computer — the phone is a mandatory relay. Everything below happens on the phone itself.

your voice ─▶ glasses mic ─▶ RayNeo cloud ASR ─▶ transcript on the phone │ ├─▶ wake gate strict match · trailing wake word · dedup ├─▶ live search weather · routes · events · prices ├─▶ model Groq or Gemini, one short sentence │ └─▶ notification ─▶ RNLink/SPP ─▶ green waveguide

Timers, to-dos and the calendar never leave the phone — they are parsed and answered locally, with no model and no network.

What this demo is not

The lens panel is an emulation, not a capture. The iO's display is invisible to any camera pointed at it — that is the point of the optics — so a screenshot of what the wearer sees does not exist. The text, the character limits, the glyphs and the timings are taken from the real device; the rendering of the green is an interpretation.

Say the wake word at the end of a sentence. The glasses' voice detection truncates the beginning of utterances, so a leading “Jarvis” is often eaten while a trailing one survives.
